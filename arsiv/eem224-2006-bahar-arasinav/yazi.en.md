## Position-Dependent Dielectric
Between the plates y=1 and y=2, ε(y)=ε₀y², V₀=4 V, S=1 m². D=8ε₀ây, E=8ây/y², P=8ε₀(1−1/y²)ây. ρpv=−16ε₀/y³; the lower polarization surface charge is zero, and the upper one is 6ε₀. Volume charge −6ε₀, surface charge 6ε₀. The free charge density on the lower plate is 8ε₀; C=2ε₀, energy 16ε₀ J.

## Dielectric Boundary
z<0: ε₁=4ε₀, z≥0: ε₂=ε₀. D₁=ε₀(3âz−4âx). Using continuity of tangential E and normal D, E₁=¾âz−âx, E₂=3âz−âx; D₂=ε₀(3âz−âx), P₁=ε₀(9âz/4−3âx), P₂=0. Angles with the normal are θ₁≈53.13°, θ₂≈18.43°; the polarization surface charge is 9ε₀/4.

*Note: In some lines of the solution page, the common ε₀ factor of D₁ has been dropped; the units from the question sheet were taken as the basis and are shown explicitly here.*

## Half-Disk Surface Charge
On the surface 0≤r≤b=3, 0≤φ≤π in the xy plane, ρs=15ε₀. There is a charge Q at z=−1 and a charge q=2 C at z=4. The force of the surface charge on q is asked, along with the Q that makes the z component of the total force zero. The source is dS′=r′dr′dφ′; R=4âz−r′cosφ′âx−r′sinφ′ây is set up. The solution gives Ez=3/4 and Q=−75πε₀ C.

*Note: For a half-disk, the φ integral is π. Since 2π was used in the source Ez calculation, the result comes out twice as large; under the same conditions, Ez=3/8 V/m and Q=−75πε₀/2 C. In addition, the sign of the y component should be checked against the direction of repulsion from the positive surface charge.*

## Field and Charge from Potential
V=2xy+y²z+x²z, εr=x. E=−(2y+2xz)âx−(2x+2yz)ây−(x²+y²)âz. At (2,1,2), E=−10âx−8ây−5âz. D=ε₀xE; ρv=−ε₀(2y+6xz), which is −26ε₀ at the same point. P=ε₀(x−1)E. The source finds ρpv=ε₀(2y+6xz−4z), giving 18ε₀ at the point.
