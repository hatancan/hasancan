EEM211 Digital Logic Design: Laboratory Information Form (2012)

EEM211 – Sayısal Mantık Tasarımı · 28.09.2012 · 16.10.2026

This post is the laboratory information form for the Başkent University Electrical-Electronics Engineering course EEM 211 Digital Logic Design (September 2012). The course instructor is Assoc. Prof. Dr. Hamit Erdem, and the laboratory supervisors are Research Assistant Gencer Tulay and Research Assistant Tunç İlgar. At that time, the experiments were done with schematic design and simulation in the Xilinx development environment and then loaded onto the FPGA board; the experiment handouts in the archive were also prepared according to this form. The original PDF of the form can be downloaded at the end of the post.

A) How the laboratory is run

  1. How the laboratory is run is explained in this document.
  2. Each experiment consists of a laboratory session held during laboratory hours.
  3. The distribution of the grades you will receive from each experiment and the related explanations are given in the GRADING section.
  4. The work schedule to be followed throughout the semester is given in the EXPERIMENT SCHEDULE section. Changes may be made to this schedule in line with the requests of the course instructor or according to changes made by the department head's office.
  5. Each student will perform the experiments individually. Otherwise, the pairs to be matched are determined by the laboratory assistant.
  6. Students must check at the start of the experiment that the computer and equipment they use are working. They must report any faulty computer or equipment to the research assistant in charge of the laboratory before starting the experiment.
  7. A quiz may be given during the experiment session without prior notice. If a quiz is given, the quiz grades make up 50% of the Performance and Oral grade.
  8. During and/or at the end of the experimental work, students must show their completed work to the assistant on duty in the laboratory. The responsible research assistant records the work completed by each student. When deemed necessary, the student may be asked questions about their work, or changes to the work may be requested.
  9. At the end of the experimental work, each student hands over the experimental setup, equipment, materials, and any documents to the assistant on duty. Students are responsible for leaving the laboratory equipment and the experiment desk in an orderly state.
  10. Throughout the semester, students are granted one make-up session, provided they have an official excuse approved by the department. The laboratory supervisors must be contacted for the time of the make-up experiment.

Note: In the form, the items of this section are numbered 11 and 12 after 8 (there are no 9 and 10); here they are numbered sequentially.

B) General rules

  1. Students must be present at the place where the experiment will be held at the experiment time. Students who arrive at the laboratory within the first 10 minutes of the experiment may enter the class with the permission of the laboratory assistants. Students who are more than 10 minutes late are not admitted to that session. If there is one, they may come at the start of the second session and do their experiments. No extra time is given to these students to complete the experiment.
  2. Walking around inside the laboratory and leaving without permission during the experiment is prohibited.
  3. Eating and drinking anything inside the laboratory is prohibited.
  4. Each student is responsible for the computer, experiment set, and materials they use.
  5. Students who do not return the laboratory materials at the end of the experiment, or who leave without clearing the experiment desk or shutting down their computer, will have 10 points deducted from the total of the relevant experiment grades.
  6. Students who cheat or let others cheat in any way will receive a grade of zero for the relevant experiments.

C) Recommendations and advice

Save the work you do during the experiment frequently to an external storage medium (such as a floppy disk, flash drive, or external hard drive). The reasons for this are:

  • The computers in the laboratories have no hardware that protects them against power outages.
  • The computers in the laboratories run software that restores the system to its original state when the computer is restarted, or in case of a power outage, a computer freeze, or the computer being turned off and on. Therefore, even if you have saved your work to the computer's hard disk, all the data you saved will be erased if you encounter such a situation.

Keep the work you do during the experiments until the end of the semester. It will be useful in some of your later experiments and/or work.
