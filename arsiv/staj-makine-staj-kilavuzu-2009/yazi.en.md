## Internships

Mechanical Engineering students complete **3 internships** during their studies:

- **MAK 200 (workshop internship):** Done within the university, for at most 10 working days; it is carried out by every student moving from the first year to the second year in the workshops and laboratories on the Bağlıca campus. The aim is to introduce students to turning, bench work (fitting), milling, welding, and measurement techniques, to teach them working and safety rules, and to give them their first experience in the production process. Students produce parts whose shapes and dimensions are given on job forms; each part is evaluated by the laboratory supervisor. Students provide their own work clothing (overalls, gloves, goggles); the total hourly leave cannot exceed 4 hours. The workshop report cannot exceed 15 pages, is submitted spiral-bound, and is handed in during the second week of the semester following the internship.
- **MAK 300:** Done outside the university, for at least 20 working days; it is carried out after 4 semesters of coursework have been completed. It is an internship that examines production technologies and the units supporting production: the production process, turning, drilling, milling, grinding, CNC machines, welding, heat treatment and casting operations, measuring instruments, ventilation and air conditioning, transportation systems, occupational safety, automatic control and robot systems, etc. are examined; opinions are given on the duties of mechanical engineers in these enterprises.
- **MAK 400:** Done outside the university, for at least 20 working days; it is carried out after 6 semesters of coursework have been completed. At production and project sites, product design, project development, and the organization and management of the enterprise are examined: occupational safety, quality control systems, production planning and control, thermal and electrical systems, maintenance and repair, CAD-CAM, and R&D if present. A student who has not passed MAK 300 cannot do MAK 400.

## Internship process (MAK 300/400)

- Internships are normally done during the summer break; the 20 working days must be uninterrupted and must not overlap with courses or exams.
- Finding an internship site is the student's responsibility; an internship done at a place not approved by the department internship committee is invalid. The student applies with a petition (Appendix-3), and once approved receives the official letter addressed to the workplace and a blank evaluation form.
- The process is as follows: starting at the latest by the end of the fall semester, find a suitable internship site; obtain approval with the petition; obtain the workplace letter with the approved petition and deliver it to the workplace; if the workplace accepts, complete the internship in the manner described in the guide; register for the MAK 300/400 course; ensure that the workplace's approved evaluation form reaches the department; submit the report by the date announced within the first three weeks of the semester.
- A student who does not submit the report in the 2nd week of the semester, or who does not get the workplace form to the department by the beginning of the 12th week of the relevant semester, is considered to have failed the internship; the obligation to monitor whether the form has arrived rests with the student.
- In choosing a workplace, the following are not considered suitable: workplaces without a corporate identity of a certain size, those oriented solely toward marketing, those lacking at least one of the project/design/production/research functions, and activities unrelated to mechanical engineering or of a purely course-like nature. During the internship, the committee may inspect students at their workplaces.
- Attendance is mandatory; the total hourly leave cannot exceed 8 hours, and if absence with a medical report exceeds 2 working days, the internship is extended by that amount.

## Courses and objections

Each internship is registered as the relevant course (MAK 200, MAK 300, MAK 400) in the semester following its completion; grades are given as "Y" (successful) or "Z" (unsuccessful); objections are made in writing within one week. A student who does not repeat an unsuccessful internship cannot graduate.
