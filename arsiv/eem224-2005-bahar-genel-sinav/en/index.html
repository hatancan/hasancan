EEM224 Electromagnetics I: 2005 Spring: Midterm Exam and Solutions

EEM224 – Elektromanyetik I · · 17.10.2026

This archive record, transcribed to text from photographed pages, contains the question texts and the legible solution steps. The photographs are kept in a private archive; there is no downloadable file.

Magnetic Coaxial Structure

The inner cylindrical shell at a=1 m carries current I in the +z direction, and the outer shell at r=2 carries the opposite current I. Between them μ=μ₀r²; in the other regions it is μ₀. For r<1 and r>2, H=B=0. For 1<r<2, H=Iâφ/(2πr), B=μ₀Irâφ/(2π).

M=(r²−1)Iâφ/(2πr), Jm=Iâz/π. At r=1 the equivalent surface current is zero; at r=2 it is −3Iâz/(4π). The equivalent volume current is 3I and that of the surface is −3I. The energy per unit length is 3μ₀I²/(8π), and the inductance is 3μ₀/(4π). The pressure is given in the source in the +r direction.

Note: The force of the current in the pressure line is unclear in the handwriting; the relation p=½(μ₂−μ₁)Hₜ² requires the square of the current.

Magnetic Boundary

At the boundary x=0, μr₁=4, μr₂=8, Js=5ây. H₂=4âx+5ây+3âz. From the normal B and tangential H conditions, H₁=8âx+5ây+8âz; B₁=μ₀(32âx+20ây+32âz).

Balance of Electric and Magnetic Forces

The field of an infinite wire along the y direction at (2,0,0) is B=−μ₀Iâz/(4π). For a line charge ρl=4πε₀ passing parallel to z through (x₀,y₀)=(2,1), E=−2ây V/m. A charge q=1 C passes in the +x direction at 10⁷ m/s. From the condition q(v×B+E)=0, I=2 A.

Cylindrical Conducting Dielectric

a=1 m, b=2 m, L=5/(4π) m; ε=2ε₀r, σ=2/r, V₀=2 V. From current continuity, J=Iâr/(2πrL), E=Iâr/5. From the voltage integral, I=10 A.

  • J=4âr/r, E=2âr, D=4ε₀râr.
  • R=0.2 Ω; the free charge density on the inner surface is 4ε₀.
  • Total charge is 10ε₀, C=5ε₀.
  • The surface polarization charge at r=2 is 6ε₀.
  • The pressure at r=1 is 4ε₀ N/m², in the +r direction.

Concepts

The last page asks, in fill-in-the-blank form, about gradient, divergence, curl, equipotentiality in a conductor, ∇·B=0, continuity of normal J, and the static Maxwell equations.

Note: The jump in normal D, rather than the normal electric field, gives the free surface charge; the source fill-in-the-blank statement does not make this distinction.
