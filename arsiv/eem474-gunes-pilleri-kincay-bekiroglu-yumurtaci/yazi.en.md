## Photovoltaic conversion

A solar cell converts incident radiation into electrical energy within a semiconductor structure. The electron–hole pairs generated by photons are separated by the electric field in the junction region; when an external circuit is connected, current and power are obtained.

Doping silicon with a pentavalent impurity such as phosphorus produces n-type material, and doping it with a trivalent impurity produces p-type material. When the p and n regions are joined, the displacement of carriers creates the junction field. The cell is shown with a protective glass cover, an anti-reflective coating, front/back contacts, and semiconductor layers.

## Cell, module, and array

When cells are connected in series, the voltage is increased; when connected in parallel, the current is increased. Modules are built from cells, and larger panels or arrays are built from modules. The presentation gives the voltage of a single cell as approximately 0.5 V. The required power, the number of connections, and the system configuration determine the selection.

## Cell types

Monocrystalline and polycrystalline silicon, thin-film, amorphous silicon, cadmium telluride, and copper indium diselenide cells are compared. The choice of technology is related to efficiency, area, cost, and operating conditions.

## Electrical equivalent circuit

A light-dependent current source, a diode, a series resistance Rs, and a parallel (shunt) resistance Rsh are used together. The currents in the presentation are named Iph (photocurrent), Id (diode current), Ish (shunt branch current), and IL (load current). The series resistance represents internal conduction and connection losses.

## I–V and P–V curves

At short circuit the voltage is zero and the current is Isc; at open circuit the current is zero and the voltage is Voc. Since power is P = VI, the maximum power lies between these two end points. As irradiance increases, the generated current and power increase. A change in cell temperature shifts the voltage and the maximum power point. The operating point of the load does not always coincide with the maximum power the cell can deliver.

## System connections

In a grid-connected system, the DC energy is converted by an inverter into AC suitable for the grid. A residential unit can meet its own consumption; large generation plants can feed the grid directly. In a stand-alone system, batteries are provided for periods without sunlight. A charge controller limits overcharging/over-discharging; an inverter is added for AC loads.

Fixed and sun-tracking mechanical arrangements are compared. It is stated that some systems use a maximum power point tracker (MPPT). This section does not give the detailed MPPT algorithm.
