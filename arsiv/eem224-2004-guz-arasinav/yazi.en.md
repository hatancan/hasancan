## Plane Wave in Free Space
The electric field is along +z and the direction of propagation is +x; the amplitude is E₀. Source phasors: Ẽ=âzE₀exp(−jk₀x), H̃=−ây(E₀/η₀)exp(−jk₀x). The instantaneous electric field is E=âzE₀cos(ωt−k₀x); the average Poynting vector is âxE₀²/(2η₀).

## Good Conductor Approximation
μ=4μ₀, ε=36ε₀, σ=2 S/m; E=10exp(−αz)cos(π×10⁸t−βz)âx V/m. σ/(ωε)=20≫1. Source: α≈β≈4π√10; λ=1/(2√10) m, δ=1/(4π√10) m. Phase velocity ω/β.

*Note: The value α≈β≈4π√10 was calculated using the good conductor approximation; since σ/(ωε)=20 in this problem, a small difference arises compared with the exact lossy-medium expressions.*

## Obliquely Propagating Wave
The phase factor in free space is exp[−j(8y+6z)]. |k|=10 rad/m, the unit propagation vector is 0.8ây+0.6âz; the angle with the z axis is approximately 53°. v=c, λ=0.2π m, f=3×10⁹/(2π) Hz. With the E field along x, the H direction is 0.6ây−0.8âz. With the amplitudes in the source, H̃=2×10⁻⁴(0.6ây−0.8âz)exp[−j(8y+6z)].

## Polarization
The last page sketches a circular example with equal amplitudes and a 90° phase difference; an elliptical example with components of amplitude 1 and 4; and another circular example with components cos(ωt−kz+π/4) and sin(ωt−kz+π/4). The directions of rotation are determined by tracing the motion of the tip of the electric field at a fixed position.
