INTERNSHIP Summer Internship: Başkent University Electrical-Electronics Engineering Internship Guide (EEM300/EEM400)

STAJ – Yaz Stajı · 01.11.2007 · 14.11.2026

This post is the text of the Başkent University Faculty of Engineering Department of Electrical-Electronics Engineering Internship Guide (November 2007, 15 pages). The guide contains the Faculty of Engineering Internship Directive and the department's rules for the EEM300 (Internship-I) and EEM400 (Internship-II) courses; its appendices include sample forms for the application petition, the letter written to the workplace, the report cover page, and the evaluation form. The department guide is the department's own document. The original PDF can be downloaded at the end of the post. For a presentation of the same process, see the post Internship Practice presentation; for sample reports, see the post TCDD EBOM internship report.

1. Faculty of Engineering Internship Directive

According to the directive, summer internships are an important part of engineering education that must be completed outside the university; the aim is for students, who have knowledge in theoretical and laboratory settings, to become acquainted with working life and with the natural, real face of production. The main articles are:

  • Duration: Throughout their studies, each student does at least 2 internships, each of at least 20 working days (Article 2). Departments may additionally require a shorter workshop internship. At least 2 semesters of courses must have been taken before the first internship.
  • Expectations: In the first internship, the student is expected to become acquainted with working life and to adapt to workplace discipline by taking an actual role in production/service facilities and workshops; in the second internship, the student is expected to get to know institutional structures and grasp the importance of institutional organization (Article 3).
  • Internship committee: Each department establishes a committee of at least 3 faculty members, one of whom is the chair, assigned by the department head (Article 4). The department prepares its own internship guide (Article 5).
  • Internship placement: The responsibility for finding a suitable internship placement belongs to the student; the placement found is finalized with the approval of the internship committee, and work done at an unapproved workplace does not count as an internship (Articles 6 and 8). Only one of the internships may be done at universities and affiliated institutions (Article 7).
  • Report and form: The student prepares an internship report in the format given in the guide and submits it no later than the 3rd week of the semester following the internship. The Internship Evaluation Form, filled out by the workplace, is delivered to the department in a sealed envelope stamped by the workplace (Articles 9 and 10).
  • Evaluation: Each internship corresponds to one course; if the course is not registered for, the internship is not evaluated. Reports and forms are reviewed by the committee and converted into a grade of "successful" (Y) or "unsuccessful" (Z); results are announced by the 12th week of the following semester; objections are made within one week (Articles 12–14).
  • Graduation and payment: A student who does not fulfill the internship obligations is not issued graduation-related documents (Article 16). The university does not pay students for internships (Article 17).
  • The directive came into effect at the beginning of the 2003-2004 Spring semester.

2. Internships and the procedure to follow

  • EEM Department students do 2 internships, each of at least 20 working days and at different workplaces.
  • The first is EEM300, done after 4 semesters of courses have been taken; the second is EEM400, done after 6 semesters of courses. A student who does not pass EEM300 cannot do EEM400.
  • Internships are normally done during the summer break; the 20 working days must be uninterrupted and must not overlap with course/exam periods.
  • The student applies to the department head's office by petition (Appendix-1) for the workplace they found; for those approved, an official letter written to the workplace and a blank evaluation form are provided (Appendix-2, Appendix-4).
  • Procedure: starting no later than the end of the fall semester, find a suitable internship placement; obtain approval by petition; with the approved petition, obtain the workplace's official internship letter and deliver it to the workplace; if the workplace accepts, complete the internship; register for the EEM300/EEM400 course; ensure that the workplace's approved evaluation form reaches the department; submit the internship report in the first three weeks of the semester.
  • A student who does not submit the report in the 3rd week, or whose workplace evaluation form does not reach the department by the beginning of the 12th week of the relevant semester, is considered unsuccessful in the internship. The obligation to monitor whether the form has reached the department belongs to the student; a form brought by hand must be in a sealed original envelope.

3. Choosing a workplace

  • The student is expected to work like an employee, abiding by all the conditions and rules of the workplace.
  • Workplaces that do not have a corporate identity of a certain size, that are purely marketing-oriented, or that lack at least one of the project, design, production, or research functions and facilities are not considered suitable.
  • Activities unrelated to the electrical-electronics profession, that involve no actual work, or that are merely course-like in nature, do not count as internships.

4. Attendance

  • Within the limited period of 20 working days, continuous attendance is required; daily leave cannot be taken, and hourly leave may be used in cases of great necessity, with a total not exceeding 8 hours.
  • Absence due to illness is possible only with a report from a fully equipped hospital or health center. If the reported period exceeds a total of 2 working days, the internship is extended by that amount; otherwise the internship is considered invalid and unsuccessful.

5. Internships and questions to be answered

5.1 The EEM300 internship

This is the students' first internship; the aim is for them to get to know a workplace involving one or more applications such as R&D, design, production, assembly, and maintenance-repair, and to gain insight and experience by actually working there. The questions to be answered are:

  1. Information about the organization where the internship was done (at most 2 pages): its name and address; a brief history; the parent organization and facilities it is affiliated with, if any; number of employees (workers, technicians, engineers, administrative staff); its status and capital structure; fields of activity.
  2. The organizational chart of the enterprise; which units perform the main functions, relationships between units, authorities and responsibilities.
  3. If production or assembly is carried out: products, inputs, domestic/foreign parts ratio, the role of design, production volumes, production stages, and the equipment used.
  4. The organization's level of use of computer systems.
  5. The main items of revenue sources and, if available, their percentages.

5.2 The EEM400 internship

This is the second internship; in addition to what is envisaged for EEM300, the aim is to get to know a workplace that has reached a certain size and completed its institutionalization, and to grasp the sector and sectoral relationships. In addition to those in EEM300, the questions include the following:

  • After-sales support and other customer services
  • R&D activities: their branches, goals, personnel structure, and technical facilities
  • Cooperation or partnership with foreign organizations
  • Position within the sector, revenue sources, main competitors, and market shares
  • Level of use of computer systems

6. Internship report

  • Reports are prepared in accordance with the guide; those that do not comply are not evaluated and the internship is considered unsuccessful.
  • A4 white paper; 2.2 cm margins at the top, bottom, and right, and 3 cm at the left; 12-point Times New Roman; single line spacing within paragraphs; at most 15 pages excluding appendices.
  • Sections are numbered 1., 2., 3., and subsections 1.1, 1.2; at most 3 levels (2.1.1) are used, and main headings are written in capital letters.
  • The report begins with the cover page in Appendix-3; after the "Table of Contents" it includes the following sections: 1. The Internship Workplace, 2. Work Done During the Internship (in chronological order, with subsections by the departments worked in), 3. Benefits Gained from the Internship (what was learned, and criticisms if any). Appendices are separated as APPENDIX-1, APPENDIX-2; unnecessary documents are not included.
  • The report should be spiral-bound along the edge if possible; otherwise it is presented in a folder.

7. Course registration

Since the internship is considered a compulsory course, EEM300 must be taken for the first internship and EEM400 for the second internship in the first semester's registration following the end of the internship. The internship of a student who does not register on time is graded at the end of the first semester in which they register; the student is responsible for any postponement of graduation.

8. Objections to evaluation results and graduation

Objections are made by written application within the period specified in the regulations; they are decided by the department board after obtaining the opinion of the department internship committee. A student who does not receive a "successful" grade in both EEM300 and EEM400 cannot graduate unless they redo the internship and are found successful.

Appendices

The appendices of the guide are as follows: Appendix-1 application petition (the student, the organization where the internship will be done, and the approval of the student's advisor and the internship committee chair), Appendix-2 A general letter and Appendix-2 B special letter (the letter written to the workplace on behalf of the department head), Appendix-3 internship report cover page (EEM 300/400 internship report; fields for the student's name, number, name of the institution where the internship was done, address, internship dates, telephone, and website), and Appendix-4 the evaluation form filled out by the workplace. The appendices are blank sample forms; they are included in the downloadable PDF.
