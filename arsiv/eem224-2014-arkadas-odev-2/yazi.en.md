## Basic operations
In the triangle drawing, using A+B=C, it is written that A+B+C=2C and A+B−C=0. For parallel vectors in the same direction, A·B=|A||B| and A×B=0; for perpendicular vectors, A·B=0 and |A×B|=|A||B|. In an orthogonal basis, |F|=√(F₁²+F₂²+F₃²), and the unit direction is F/|F|.

*Note: With the definition "a closed triangle with each end added to the next start", if all sides follow the same circulation direction, the total is A+B+C=0; in the source drawing, C has been chosen as the resultant of the other two sides. Also, in the expression (A·B)×C the first term is a scalar, so the cross product is not defined; the source treats it as meaningful.*

## Numerical vector example
A=(1,2,−3), B=(0,−4,1), C=(5,0,−2). The source writes |A−B|=√53; the unit vector in the direction of A is A/√14. For A·B it gives −10, and for the angle approximately 130.35°; the cross product and triple product lines also contain successive arithmetic errors.

## Parallel fields
For two vector fields that are parallel at every point, a common scalar ratio of the form A=kB is required; the components are expected to satisfy the same ratio.

## Electric field from potential
For V(R)=E₀Rsinθ, E=−∇V=−E₀(âRsinθ+âθcosθ). At the point R=1, θ=60°, φ=30°, E=−E₀[(√3/2)âR+(1/2)âθ].
