EEM323 Electromagnetics II: Course Introduction and Fall 2013 Syllabus (Prof. Dr. S. Gökhun Tanyer)

EEM323 – Elektromanyetik II · 19.09.2013 · 14.11.2026

This post brings together the course syllabus for the 2013–2014 fall semester of EEM323 Electromagnetics II (Electromagnetic Wave Theory II) at Başkent University and the two-page introduction document by the course instructor, Prof. Dr. S. Gökhun Tanyer. The documents are dated 18–19 September 2013. The original PDFs can be downloaded in the ZIP file at the end of the post.

Although the folder name is "Electrical Machines", the documents refer to the course as "EEM 323 Electromagnetics II"; the name used in the documents has been kept in the archive.

Purpose and scope of the course

The EEM224 Electromagnetics I course covered time-invariant (static) electric and magnetic fields. EEM323 is its continuation and uses the same textbooks: static fields are briefly summarized, and then the main question is addressed: what happens when there is a change in time? When the charge distribution changes or the current increases and decreases over time, the assumption that electric and magnetic fields are "static" is no longer valid. The syllabus states that the work of Faraday, Ampère, Kirchhoff and especially Maxwell on this subject will be examined through examples.

Course schedule and grading

  • Semester: 23 September 2013 – 3 January 2014; final exam week begins 5 January.
  • Class hours: Monday 13:00–14:50, Thursday 11:00–12:50. Office hours: Monday 15:00–16:50.
  • Grade breakdown: 10% homework, 10% quizzes, 35% midterm, 45% final.
  • Homework is assigned weekly and is due the following Monday; late submissions lose 20% of the homework grade for every 24 hours. No make-up exam is planned.
  • Prerequisites: MAT151, MAT152, EEM224; basic algebra, complex analysis and Matlab-like programming.

Textbooks

  1. David K. Cheng, Fundamentals of Engineering Electromagnetics (Addison-Wesley, 1993; Turkish translation by Palme Yayınları).
  2. David K. Cheng, Field and Wave Electromagnetics (Turkish translation by Literatür Yayıncılık).
  3. Marshall, DuBroff, Skitek, Electromagnetic Concepts and Applications, 4th edition, Prentice Hall, 1996.

Objectives

According to the syllabus, a student completing the course will grasp the physical interpretation of Maxwell's equations, appreciate the importance of mathematical approximations in simplifying nature, be able to describe the similarities and differences between the electromagnetic and optical wave equations, describe light as an electromagnetic wave, explain the relationship between a ray and a wavefront, describe the conditions for interference (constructive/destructive) and the use of a polarizer/analyzer, and unify the knowledge of electric and magnetic fields at 0 Hz with optical waves on the order of terahertz.

14-week topic plan (draft)

WeekTopics
1Introduction; vector algebra and operators; summary of static electric and magnetic fields
2Time-varying fields; Faraday's law
3Stationary circuit in a time-varying magnetic field; transformers; moving conductor in a static magnetic field
4Maxwell's equations (differential and integral form); potential functions
5Solution of wave equations; time-harmonic fields, phasors; Helmholtz equations
6Plane electromagnetic waves; Doppler effect; wave polarization
7Plane waves in lossy media
8Midterm exam
9Electromagnetic power; Poynting vector; instantaneous and average power density
10Plane waves at normal incidence on planar boundaries; normal incidence on a good conductor
11Oblique incidence; perpendicular and parallel polarization; Brewster angle
12Transmission lines; general equations and parameters
13Wave characteristics on infinite and finite transmission lines; open- and short-circuited lines
14Reflection coefficient; standing wave ratio; Smith chart
15Final exam

Introduction of the instructor

Prof. Dr. S. Gökhun Tanyer teaches EEM323 Electromagnetics II, EEM423 Antenna Theory and Electromagnetic Propagation, and EEM549 Optical Wave Theory in the Department of Electrical and Electronics Engineering at Başkent University. The introduction page lists the research topics as follows: digital signal processing (adaptive-resolution time-frequency analysis, audio processing), statistics (test statistics, random number generators), antenna design and optimization (antenna arrays; genetic, PSO and fuzzy methods), and music theory (the natural-37 interval tone system, makams, Al-Jurjani's 1000-year-old problem, the fifth–octave conflict). The page also includes the weekly course schedule and graduate thesis topics.
