The text content of three lecture notes that Prof. Dr. S. Gökhun Tanyer gave at the start of the **EEM 323 Electromagnetics II** course: a review of Electromagnetics I (EEM 224), coordinate systems, and the mathematical operators needed in electromagnetics. The notes belong to the 2013–2014 fall semester (October–November 2013). Since almost all of the equations and figures in the notes are taken from the textbook, only the instructor's headings, explanations and assignments are included here.

## 1. Electromagnetics I review (static electric and magnetic fields)

The 26-slide review recalls the topics of "static E and H fields" in order. The flow of the lecture is as follows:

- **Vector algebra:** addition, subtraction, the cross product, products of three vectors; orthogonal coordinate systems (Cartesian, cylindrical, spherical).
- **Vector and scalar integrals:** the gradient of a scalar field, the divergence (flux) and the curl (rotation) of a vector field.
- **Sources of the electric field:** Coulomb's law and the electric force; Gauss's law and its applications; electric potential.
- **Static electric field in matter:** conductors, dielectrics, equivalent charge distributions of polarized dielectrics, electric flux density (D), permittivity.
- **Boundary conditions, capacitance and capacitors;** electrostatic energy and forces; torque.
- **Laplace's and Poisson's equations;** the method of images; boundary-value problems.
- **Current:** current density, Ohm's law, resistance, electromotive force, Kirchhoff's voltage and current laws, Joule's law (power dissipation), boundary conditions for current density, resistance calculations.
- **Magnetostatics:** vector magnetic potential, the Biot–Savart law and its applications, magnetic dipole, scalar magnetic potential, magnetization and equivalent current densities, magnetic field intensity (H) and relative permeability.
- **Magnetic circuits,** behavior of magnetic materials, magnetostatic boundary conditions, inductance and inductors, magnetic energy, magnetic force.

The slides link each topic with the memory of a scientist: Coulomb (1736–1806), Gauss (1777–1855), Laplace (1749–1827) and Poisson (1781–1840), Ohm (1789–1854), Kirchhoff (1824–1887), Joule (1818–1889), Biot (1774–1862) and Savart (1791–1841), Ampère (1775–1836).

## 2. Coordinate systems

The purpose of the note is to summarize the three orthogonal coordinate systems used in electromagnetic problems:

- **Cartesian** (x, y, z) or (i, j, k),
- **Cylindrical** (r, φ, z),
- **Spherical** (R, θ, φ); R is Cheng's preferred notation, while other books use r.

The slides show, in turn, the unit vectors of each system, the differential length/surface/volume elements, and the **transformations between systems** (these are figures and equations scanned from the book, so they are not given here).

## 3. Operators (gradient, divergence, curl)

Three operators needed in electromagnetic theory:

| Operator | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gradient | Direction and rate of increase of a scalar field |
| Divergence | Amount of divergence of a vector field (outflow from a source) |
| Curl (rotational) | Rotational structure of a vector field |

Topics covered in the note:

1. Computing the curl of a vector field (in Cartesian coordinates).
2. **Stokes's theorem**: the equality of the curl integral over a surface to the circulation integral along the closed path bounding the surface; two worked examples and three homework questions.
3. **Helmholtz's theorem:** the classification of vector fields according to their divergence and curl properties (below).

The Helmholtz classification has four groups:

- a field with zero divergence and zero curl,
- a field with zero divergence but nonzero curl (solenoidal),
- a field with nonzero divergence but zero curl (irrotational),
- a field with both nonzero divergence and nonzero curl. The instructor's example: the electric field in a medium containing charge, together with a time-varying magnetic field, falls into this last class.
