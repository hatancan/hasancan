This content from the Başkent University EEM332 Microprocessors course archive was transcribed to text from photographed pages. It is a text transcription of the PIC16F84 circuit board in the Pickart presentation, covering its LED and seven-segment options and power supply connections. Student names and numbers have been removed. The photographs are kept in a private archive; this post has no download file.

## Board Sections

The source presentation is titled "Application circuit — PIC 16F84". It shows the front and back of the printed circuit board labeled Başkent University, the component layout, and the circuit schematic. The board has a PIC16F84 socket, four red and four green LEDs, a single-digit seven-segment display, buttons, a buzzer, a crystal, and a power supply circuit.

## Component Selection by Project

The presentation compares different setups by covering unused components with white areas. In projects that use the seven-segment display, the output arrangement with eight separate LEDs is not used. In projects that use eight LEDs, the seven-segment display and its associated display connections are not used. The jumper connections on the board are marked as "Jumper". The general circuit schematic and the layout required for each project are shown separately.

*Note: The covered areas in the photograph indicate which components will not be fitted; they do not mean that all components will be fitted at the same time for every project. Because the small resistor and connection labels could not all be read reliably, no assembly list has been produced here.*

## Power Supply

The schematic on the final slides shows a 9 V input, an ON/OFF switch, an LM7805 regulator, an output capacitor, and the +5 V rail. In the regulator drawing, the input, common, and output connections are numbered. On the other pages, the PIC, buttons, and displays are treated together with this power supply.

## Archive Scope

The board photographs and the drawings containing photographs from the presentation's 14 pages are kept in a private archive. Only the headings, markings, and legible technical information have been transcribed. The date of the post is taken from the 25 April 2014 creation record in the original presentation's metadata.
