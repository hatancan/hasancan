## Gradient, divergence, and curl
Two pages dated 17 May summarize the gradient (scalar to vector), the divergence (vector to scalar), and the curl (vector to vector). In Cartesian coordinates, ∇V = âx∂V/∂x+ây∂V/∂y+âz∂V/∂z; ∇·A = ∂Ax/∂x+∂Ay/∂y+∂Az/∂z. The expansions with metric coefficients for cylindrical and spherical coordinates are also written out.

Electric field E=−∇V. Procedure for the field of a point charge: choose the coordinate system, write dq in terms of the line/surface/volume element, determine the source and observation vectors, find the difference R−R′ and its magnitude, set up the expression dE=dq(R−R′)/(4πε₀|R−R′|³), and integrate over the source.

## Magnetic boundary conditions and Ampère's law
The 30 May pages give B=μH, the condition B₁n=B₂n, and, in the absence of surface current, H₁t=H₂t. For a circle around a line current, H=âφHφ, dl=âφr dφ. From ∮H·dl=I, it follows that Hφ=I/(2πr) and B=μI/(2πr).

## Motional electromotive force
The 16 October page has the relations EMF=∮(v×B)·dl and v=ωr; in the rotating rod example, the result is −ωB₀l²/2 for the chosen direction. For a time-varying field, Φ=∫B·dS and EMF=−dΦ/dt. If B=B₀sin(ωt) and the angle α with the field normal is constant, Φ=B₀sin(ωt)hw cosα and EMF=−B₀ωcos(ωt)hw cosα.
