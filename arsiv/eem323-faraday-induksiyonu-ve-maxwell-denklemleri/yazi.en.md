This post gives the text content of three lecture notes that Prof. Dr. S. Gökhun Tanyer covered in **EEM 323 Electromagnetics II** (Fall 2013–2014, November 11, 2013): time-varying fields, Faraday's induction, and Maxwell's equations. Because the notes consist mostly of figures and equations taken from the textbook, only their headings and the instructor's explanations are included here.

## 1. Time-varying fields

This 12-slide introductory note covers the transition from the static case to time-varying fields (its content consists entirely of figure and equation images; only its title is text). It should be read together with the next note.

## 2. Faraday's induction

The headings of the note, in order:

- **Flux-cutting emf:** the emf induced in a conductor moving within a magnetic field.
- **Motional emf.**
- **Total electromotive force (emf):** the sum of the emf due to the field changing with time (transformer emf) and the emf due to the motion of the circuit; in the examples, parts (a), (b), and (c) are computed separately, and the total emf is found in part (c).

## 3. Maxwell's equations

**Displacement current.** In the instructor's words: physical current and/or moving charges create a nonzero magnetic curl; when this is added alongside Faraday's law, and the two divergence equations are written for completeness, the "steps that generate waves" are complete. These **four equations are known as Maxwell's equations**.

**Point (differential) form.** In a source-free, linear, and isotropic region (B = μH, D = εE):

- ∇ × E = −μ ∂H/∂t
- ∇ × H = ε ∂E/∂t
- ∇ · E = 0
- ∇ · H = 0

**Integral form.** The four equations are integrated in turn over an arbitrary surface S and volume V to obtain the integral form: Faraday's law of induction, Ampère's circuital law, Gauss's law, and (in the instructor's words) the equation stating that **there are no isolated magnetic charges in the universe**.

**Electromagnetic boundary conditions.** The boundary conditions of the static case also hold for time-varying fields. Special cases: when both media are lossless and there is no source at the interface; and when medium 1 is a dielectric and medium 2 is a perfect conductor.

**Homogeneous vector wave equation.** The interdependence of E and H is presented as a "chain of field generation": if E varies with time, it produces a time-varying H, the time-varying H in turn produces E, and so on. Taking the curl of the first equation and combining it with the second yields the **homogeneous vector wave equation** for E. The speed of light in the medium is u = 1/√(με); in free space this is approximately 300,000 km/s.
