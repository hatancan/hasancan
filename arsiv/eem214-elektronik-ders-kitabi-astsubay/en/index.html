EEM214 Electronics I: A Ten-Chapter Turkish "Electronics" Textbook, from Resonance to Oscillators (Non-Commissioned Officers' School Course Notes, 2002)

EEM214 – Elektronik I · 27.02.2002 · 16.10.2026

This post introduces a ten-chapter Turkish "Electronics" textbook, found as Word files in the EEM214 Electronics I course folder, together with its table of contents page. The author of the book is not stated in the document; the file properties give a date of 2002 and a computer record from a non-commissioned officers' school, and the text contains military application examples such as "radar" and "combat systems". For this reason it appears to be an internal textbook or set of course notes from a military vocational school; there is no indication that it was a commercially published book. Page numbers are given per chapter (I-1, II-1, …). The topics of the chapters are summarized below; the Word files can be downloaded as a ZIP at the end of the post.

Chapter I – Resonant Circuits

Resonance is the special condition in AC circuits containing an inductor and a capacitor in which the inductive reactance (X_L) equals the capacitive reactance (X_C); it is used in checking the operating frequencies of radar transceivers and in station tuning in radio receivers. The chapter first examines the AC behavior of passive elements (resistive, inductive, and capacitive AC circuits) and then covers series RLC resonance (the resonance condition, operation above and below resonance, bandwidth, circuit characteristics) and parallel resonance. The resonant frequency is f₀ = 1 / (2π√(LC)); above resonance the circuit behaves inductively, and below resonance it behaves capacitively.

Chapter II – Waveshaping Circuits

Control and timing functions require non-sinusoidal waveforms such as square, sawtooth, trapezoidal, rectangular, and peaked waves. Topics: the RC time constant, the charging rate of a capacitor, the time constant of an RL circuit, classification of time constants, the waveshaping process, and differentiating and integrating circuits.

Chapter III – Semiconductor Fundamentals

The history of semiconductors (the Curie brothers' crystal studies in the 1880s, the selenium rectifier, the 1930 germanium diode, the first transistor in 1948; the word "transistor" from the words transfer and resistor). Conductors, insulators, and semiconductors; the crystal structure of silicon and increasing its conductivity; the formation of N-type and P-type semiconductors and their current directions; the P-N junction and the crystal diode; forward and reverse bias; diode characteristics and the circuit for obtaining the characteristic curve; the Zener diode.

Chapter IV – Semiconductor Power Supplies

Converting the 220 V, 50 Hz AC voltage from the mains into a DC voltage suitable for electronic circuits by means of a DC power supply consisting of a transformer, rectifier, filter, and voltage regulator. Half-wave, center-tapped full-wave, and bridge rectifiers; L-type and π-type filters; voltage limiters; voltage doublers and triplers; simple clipper and clamper circuits; regulators with Zener diodes.

Chapter V – Transistors

Transistor structures (point-contact, junction, alloy, and alloy-diffused methods); PNP and NPN junction transistors; control of the collector current by the base current in the NPN; transistor symbols; forward and reverse biasing of PNP and NPN transistors.

Chapter VI – Transistor Amplifiers

The basic block structure of an amplifier (increasing amplitude or current without changing frequency); obtaining the four-region characteristic curves of the transistor; finding the output impedance, the current gain β, and the load line; Class A, AB, B, and C operation; common-emitter and common-base circuit analysis; power amplifiers, the balanced power amplifier, phase splitters, the complementary-symmetry power amplifier; transformer, RC, direct, and impedance coupling.

Chapter VII – Special Semiconductors

Field-effect transistors: the operation and parameters of the JFET, the operation and characteristics of MOSFETs, JFET and MOSFET amplifiers; the unijunction transistor (UJT) and the UJT relaxation oscillator; the silicon-controlled rectifier (SCR); a transistorized voltage control circuit; the triac and its applications.

Chapter VIII – Special-Type Amplifiers

Narrowband (the RF amplifier in receivers) and wideband amplifiers, the square-wave characteristic, differential amplifiers, and operational amplifiers (op-amps): the inverting and non-inverting amplifier, voltage follower, summing amplifier, difference amplifier, comparator, integrator and differentiator, logarithmic amplifier, half-wave and full-wave rectifiers, voltage-controlled oscillator, low-pass, high-pass, and band-pass filters; op-amp parameters.

Chapter IX – Waveform Generation Methods

Waveform generators (oscillators) produce sine, square, rectangular, and sawtooth signals from a few hertz to several gigahertz; essentially, an oscillator is an amplifier that supplies its own input signal. Sinusoidal and non-sinusoidal oscillators; R-C (audio-frequency) and L-C oscillators; the conditions required for oscillation; crystals; astable, monostable, and bistable multivibrators.

Chapter X – Oscillators

Feedback and oscillation; transistor and op-amp phase-shift R-C oscillators, the Armstrong oscillator, series-fed and shunt-fed Hartley oscillators, transistor and op-amp Colpitts oscillators, the voltage-controlled oscillator, crystal-controlled oscillators, and the transistorized sawtooth generator.
