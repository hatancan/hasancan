Generation of Amplitude-Modulated Signals

It was seen that amplitude modulation produces new frequency components such as fc+fm and fc-fm. To produce new frequency components, the device generating the amplitude-modulated signal must have either a nonlinear or a time-varying characteristic. Linear, time-invariant elements cannot produce new frequency components.

Two methods are used: Square-Law Method (Square-Law Modulator), Switching Method (Switching Modulator). Bandpass Filter. Filtered out by the bandpass filter. Passes through the bandpass filter.

Tuned-Circuit Modulator

This method is used to obtain amplitude-modulated signals at high power levels. The modulator operates as a class C amplifier with the modulation applied at the anode. Such modulators are widely used in standard amplitude-modulated transmitters. A pentode tube or a high-power semiconductor device can be used instead of a triode.

Single Sideband (SSB) Modulation

If one of the sidebands is removed from an SC-AM signal, a single-sideband signal results. In this way, transmitter power and bandwidth are halved. Because of their small bandwidth, SSB-modulated signals are less affected by fading than AM and SC-AM signals. SSB modulation is not suitable for signals with significant low-frequency components. SSB modulation is used for radio communication by the military and police in the 2–25 MHz range. Because the receiver structure is complex and expensive, it is not used for radio broadcasting. The SSB signal is generated by two methods. Filter or Frequency-Discrimination Method. Phase-Shift or Phase-Discrimination Method.

Generation of SSB-Modulated Signals

Generating the SSB Signal by the Filter Method. This method is based on the principle of suppressing one of the sidebands of the SC-AM signal by filtering, and it is simpler than the phase-shift method. Because designing sharp-cutoff filters is easier at low frequencies, the SSB signal is generated at these frequencies and then shifted to higher frequencies. In this way, filters without sharp cutoff are sufficient to suppress the unwanted high-frequency components that lie far enough from the desired frequency components. The passband of the sideband filter must include the desired sideband, and the transition band must be at most twice the smallest frequency component of the message signal.

Since it is difficult to build highly selective filters at radio frequency, modulation is carried out in two stages.

Two methods can be applied to demodulate SSB-modulated signals.

Vestigial Sideband (VSB) Modulation

In some cases, one of the sidebands cannot be completely separated out. Therefore, SSB modulation cannot be used. VSB modulation is used for such signals. One of the sidebands is received completely and the other partially. The bandwidth lies between W and 2W. SC-AM and SSB modulations are special cases of VSB modulation. In television broadcasting, the video signal is transmitted with VSB modulation.
