## Motivation

The motivation section of the 2014 "Course Outline" document (in Turkish):

> In the course EEM 224 Electromagnetics (EM) I, under stationary/static conditions (that is, in a stationary medium where nothing changes in time), we first studied the definition of the electric field and then the magnetic field, their physical properties, and the methods for predicting and calculating how they change under different conditions.

> The course EEM 323 EM II is defined as a continuation of the preceding EEM 224 course. The same reference books are used. In terms of scope, static electric and static magnetic fields are summarized, and then the main subject, the question "what happens when something changes in time?", is answered. Examples of such changes are a change in the charge distribution, or the current increasing or decreasing with time. In both cases, the fundamental assumption that the electric and magnetic fields are "static" no longer holds.

> This historically important question was studied by many scientists, who were greatly impressed by the results they obtained. In particular, in our course we will encounter the names of Faraday, Ampère, Kirchhoff, and especially Maxwell, whose work we will examine through examples. Considering that Einstein hung the photographs of three people he regarded as the most important, Newton, Faraday, and Maxwell, we will study together the work of the period in which the most important developments in physics took place.

## Course Scope

The scope in the 2013 course planning document:

**1. Introduction**

- Scope of the course
- Its place and importance in the undergraduate Electrical and Electronics Engineering curriculum
- Introduction to the general scope, review of background knowledge that may be needed

**2. Vector algebra**

- Vector addition and subtraction, vector products
- Coordinate systems, integrals
- Gradient, divergence, curl, Stokes' theorem

**3. Static electric field**

- Fundamental laws of electrostatics in free space, Coulomb's law
- Electric potential
- Behavior of dielectric materials in a static electric field
- Electric flux density and permittivity
- Boundary conditions in a static electric field
- Capacitance and capacitors
- Electrostatic energy and forces
- Electrostatic boundary-value problems

**4. Steady electric current**

- Current density and Ohm's law; force due to the electric field and Ohm's law
- Continuity equation and Kirchhoff's current law
- Power dissipation and Joule's law
- Boundary conditions for current density, resistance calculations

**5. Static magnetic field**

- Fundamental laws of static magnetic fields in free space
- Vector magnetic potential, Biot-Savart law and its applications
- Magnetic dipole; magnetization and equivalent current density
- Magnetic field intensity and relative permeability
- Magnetic circuits, behavior of magnetic materials
- Boundary conditions in static magnetic fields
- Inductance and inductors, magnetic energy, magnetic force and torque

## Course Objectives

The objectives in the 2014 document are in English:

When you finish this course you will be able to:

- Understand the physical interpretations of Maxwell's equations
- Get a strong feeling of the meanings of the fundamental electromagnetic equations
- Realize the practical applications of mathematical approximations and see their importance in simplifying "nature" so that we (simple minds) could try to understand them
- Describe the similarities and the differences between the governing equations for an electromagnetic wave and an optical wave
- Describe "light" as an electromagnetic wave
- Describe the relationship between light rays and wave fronts
- Calculate water wave displacement on a sinusoid-like waveform as a function of time and position
- Describe how electromagnetic waves are similar to and different from water waves
- State the conditions required for producing interference patterns
- Define constructive and destructive interference
- Describe how polarizers/analyzers are used with polarized light
- Merge your internal understanding of electric and magnetic fields at 0 Hertz to optical waves above ultra-violet at very large frequencies stated in terms of terahertz...
- You would look at your own eyes and binoculars at a different angle,
- and hopefully, enjoy photography more, and you could even become an amateur in astronomy.
