## Lecture 4: Semiconductor

- The number of charge carriers that can take part in conduction in a semiconductor lies between that of a conductor and an insulator; the semiconductor device is essentially a pn junction formed by p-type and n-type silicon in contact.
- **Intrinsic silicon:** each atom shares its four valence electrons with its neighbors through covalent bonds. At room temperature, thermal ionization produces equal numbers of free electrons and holes: n = p = n_i; in silicon about 1.5 × 10¹⁰ carriers/cm³ (5 × 10²² atoms/cm³). n_i² = B·T³·e^(−E_G/kT); for silicon B = 5.4 × 10³¹, E_G = 1.12 eV, k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K.
- **Doped silicon:** donor (n-type) and acceptor (p-type) dopants, majority and minority carrier concentrations; drift and diffusion currents.

## Lecture 5: Diode

- **Physical structure and the pn junction:** diffusion, depletion region and built-in voltage in open circuit; widening of the depletion region (W_dep) and junction capacitance under reverse bias; breakdown region (Zener and avalanche); minority carrier injection and diode current under forward bias.
- **Characteristics:** forward region, reverse region and breakdown region.
- **Models:** large-signal models (ideal diode, exponential model, piecewise-linear model, constant voltage drop model) and the small-signal model; equivalent circuits for each and examples of diode circuit analysis.
- **Special diodes:** Zener diode and silicon-controlled rectifier (SCR).
- **Applications:** regulator, half-wave and full-wave rectifier, peak rectifier, limiter, clamper and diode digital logic.

## Lecture 6: MOS Field-Effect Transistors (MOSFETs)

A three-terminal device in which the voltage across two terminals controls the current in the third; the basic element of amplifier and switch design and of integrated circuits (applications: signal amplification, digital logic, memory). Structure of the enhancement-mode NMOS: p-type substrate (body), two heavily doped n-type regions (source and drain), and a polysilicon gate over a thin SiO₂ insulator; the pn junctions between the substrate and the source/drain are kept reverse biased, and the body is connected to the source so that the device is treated as three-terminal. The notes cover NMOS and PMOS characteristics, channel-length modulation (V_A), internal capacitances and a summary.

## Lecture 7: Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT)

NPN and PNP structures; operation of the NPN transistor in the active, reverse-active and saturation modes (carrier flows, minority carrier distribution in the base region), the **Ebers-Moll (EM) model**, the same modes for the PNP transistor, and a summary of the relationships among i_C, i_B and i_E in active mode (i_C = β·i_B, i_E = (β + 1)·i_B, α = β/(β + 1)), and the equivalent circuit of a saturated transistor.
