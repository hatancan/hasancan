## Franchising – dealership – licensing

Franchising is a contract-based relationship between two mutually independent parties; the contract may cover a product, a brand, or a service. Its most important feature is that the party granting the franchise (the franchisor) provides knowledge and support (know-how) in management and organization; in short, it is permission, given in return for payment to the rightsholder, to use certain industrial property rights commercially. For successful franchising, the brand must be well known, the product must have a ready market, the franchisor must provide adequate training and continuous support, it must use its franchisees first and foremost as a marketing channel, and it must have good relations with them. The presentation also describes the characteristics of dealership and licensing agreements separately.

## Import barriers

Importing is the purchase, by buyers in the country, of goods produced abroad; it is the opposite of exporting, and together with it forms the country's foreign trade balance. Two conclusions follow from the analysis of an importing country: the domestic producers of the good are worse off and its consumers are better off; because consumer gains exceed producer losses, trade increases the country's economic welfare. **Tariffs** are taxes levied on the value or quantity of imports (they originated as a way of raising revenue for the government; today their main purpose is to protect domestic firms from competition). **Quotas** limit the quantity of goods that may be imported or exported in a given period. **GATT** (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade), signed in 1947, aimed to end tariff discrimination among countries.

## Just-in-time (JIT) production

JIT is based on the principle of eliminating waste and of producing goods at the desired time, in the desired quantity and quality, and delivering them to the right place; the most fundamental point of success is determining supply quantity and timing correctly. The principles are: every production unit is both a customer and a supplier; customers and suppliers are part of the process; ways of simplifying are sought continuously; problems should be prevented rather than solved; and materials, semi-finished goods and finished goods are produced or obtained at the moment they are needed. The time from raw material input to product output consists of five stages (processing time, inspection time, transportation, waiting, …).

## Marketing engineering

Marketing engineering is an approach that combines analysis and design methods in a structured, systematic and theoretical procedure. The presentation describes electronic market platforms (market mechanisms, trading rules, platforms, media and legal frameworks) and the sections on the "market engineering framework" and the "market engineering process."

## Structural characteristics of epidemics (tipping point)

In an era when the effect of traditional marketing is waning, interpersonal communication becomes stronger: people begin to buy on the recommendation of those they trust and like. When a certain **tipping point** is reached, the product turns into a **fad** (an epidemic); if it is not reached, the product's differences remain confined to a narrow circle. The presentation examines how products, ideas, messages and behaviors spread like a virus, and touches on characteristics of epidemics such as their sudden outbreak and sudden end.

## Corn-in-a-cup businesses

Corn-in-a-cup businesses are discussed as an example of entrepreneurship that exploits a gap in the market: corn, which previously attracted little interest, is now experiencing accelerating production and rising quality thanks to this trend; raw material is easy to gather, and cost and labor requirements are low; the only prerequisite is meeting the standards that running a business requires. Developing the market network through franchising and differentiating the product are recommended; shopping malls and crowded squares are considered suitable for marketing. In addition, when investing in new sectors, acquisition principles (not paying more than the value, being objective) and four growth risks (demand, competitor, complementary, and operating risk) are described.

## Akio Morita and Sony ("Made in Japan")

A summary of Akio Morita's book *Made in Japan*: the founding of Sony, post-World War II Japan, Japanese thought and way of life, the Japanese business management philosophy and behavior patterns, a comparison of Japanese and American management styles; Morita's family and school life, his meeting with Masaru Ibuka, the Tokyo Telecommunications Research Laboratory, the name "Sonny" and its Japanese connotation of "losing money," efforts to gain a foothold in the Japanese market, color TV, handheld cameras, floppy disks, Sony Corporation of America, the Walkman. Morita's view on management: "Before a big investment, you must learn the market, know how you will sell, and only after the company has gained courage should you roll up your sleeves."

## Your best competitor is your own brand: megabranding

Megabranding is a very large brand marketing its other brands under a single roof (such as Coca-Cola, Fanta, Sprite); it draws on the power of the big brand and saves costs through centralization. The thesis of the presentation is the opposite: brands should compete with each other, have separate sales and marketing organizations, and their characteristics should not overlap; "if you don't compete fiercely with your own brand, rival firms will do the job quite well," that is, one must steal market share from one's own brands (before others do). Example: until 1993, Sprite was a brand in the soda market, and then it was positioned in the general soft drink category; the risk is entering into competition with strong brands such as Coke, Diet Coke and Fanta.

## Differentiation

"Stop trying to be perfect, try to be noticed": the P's of marketing (product, price, promotion, place, policy, public opinion) are no longer enough; differentiation is required. The point is to make money; a brand that cannot differentiate, even if known by everyone, cannot achieve the desired profitability. The presentation proceeds through the sections "the way out," differentiation strategies, and conclusion.
