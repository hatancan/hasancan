## Our Objectives

In the course EEM 224 Electromagnetics (EM) I, we studied, under static/stationary conditions (that is, in a still medium where nothing changes over time), first the electric field and then the magnetic field: their definitions, their physical properties, and the methods for predicting and calculating how they change under different conditions.

The course EEM 323 EM II is defined as a continuation of the preceding course EEM 224. The same textbooks are used. In terms of scope, it summarizes static electric and static magnetic fields and then addresses the actual subject matter: "what happens when something changes in time?" Examples of such changes include a change in the charge distribution, or the current increasing or decreasing with time. In both cases, the assumption that the electric and magnetic fields are "static" is no longer valid.

Many scientists have studied this historically important question and were deeply impressed by the results they obtained. In particular, we will encounter the names Faraday, Ampère, Kirchhoff and especially Maxwell, whose work we will examine through examples in our course. Considering that Einstein hung photographs of Newton, Faraday and Maxwell as the three most important people, we will together study the work of the period in which the most important developments in physics took place.

## Objectives

When you finish this course you will be able to:

- Understand the physical interpretations of Maxwell's equations
- Get a strong feeling of the meanings of the fundamental electromagnetic equations
- Realize the practical applications of mathematical approximations and see their importance in simplifying "nature" so that we (simple minds) could try to understand them
- Describe the similarities and the differences between the governing equations for an electromagnetic wave and an optical wave
- Describe "light" as an electromagnetic wave
- Describe the relationship between light rays and wave fronts
- Calculate water wave displacement on a sinusoid-like waveform as a function of time and position
- Describe how electromagnetic waves are similar to and different from water waves
- State the conditions required for producing interference patterns
- Define constructive and destructive interference
- Describe how polarizers/analyzers are used with polarized light
- Merge your internal understanding of electric and magnetic fields at 0 Hertz to optical waves above ultra-violet at very large frequencies stated in terms of terahertz...
- You would look at your own eyes and binoculars at a different angle,
- and hopefully, enjoy photography more, and you could even become an amateur in astronomy.

## Projects

Single person projects for first and second half of semester.

- Two projects will be assigned.
- Topics will include vector algebra, modelling of wave dynamics (plots and graphs), simulation of wave dynamics (images, videos, sound).
- Matlab of any version or similar software programming packages can be used.
- The projects will be accepted in project format which will include: title page (name/surname, name of the project, summary); theoretical details (you will explain what you had accomplished and what is your output); numerical results and figures; source code written in text format.
- A list of project topics will be listed. Projects will be assigned to each student as "first come first serve".
- First project: weeks 1 – 9 (last lecture in that week).
- Second project: weeks 10 – 14 (last lecture in that week).
