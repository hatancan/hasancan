This post was prepared from the 2004 fall semester Electromagnetics exam and solution pages; it was transcribed to text from photographed pages. The source gives the old course code as EEM323; it is classified under EEM224 in the archive. Personal information was not transcribed. ## Scope of the solution pages

Types A and B each have three handwritten solution pages. The first problem deals with reflection at a perfect-conductor boundary for an obliquely incident wave, and the second computes normal incidence and the medium parameters. Since the question sheets are not included in this group, the data visible in the solutions has been transcribed.

## Type A: Oblique Incidence

For the wave with phase term 8πz+6πy, β=√[(8π)²+(6π)²]=10π rad/m, f=1.5 GHz in free space, and λ=0.2 m. From the geometric ratios, sinθ_i=0.8, cosθ_i=0.6, and θ_i≈53° are found.

The field is decomposed into components perpendicular and parallel to the plane of incidence. The relation H̃=(1/η₀)â_k×Ẽ is used. For a perfect conductor, the transmitted field is zero; the reflected components are chosen with signs that make the total tangential electric field vanish at the boundary. The time-domain expression of the phasor is obtained with Re{Ẽe^(jωt)}.

## Type A: Normal Incidence

The solution finds Γ=1/4 and τ=1+Γ=5/4. From the impedance relation, ε_r=9/25 is obtained, and the ratio between the wave constants is 3/5. The time-average Poynting vector is:

S_av=½Re{Ẽ×H̃*}.

The incident, reflected, and transmitted powers are computed, and the directional power balance is checked.

## Type B: Oblique Incidence

For Type B, the same values β=10π rad/m, f=1.5 GHz, and λ=0.2 m are found. The angle of incidence is approximately 37°. In the solution, the perpendicular component amplitude is taken as 5, and the Cartesian components of the parallel component are taken as 3 and 4. The incident and reflected fields are computed separately and summed; the tangential E is zero at the conductor surface.

## Type B: Normal Incidence

From the given power ratio, |Γ|²=1/25, hence |Γ|=1/5. For the transition into the medium with the larger impedance, the positive sign is chosen, giving Γ=1/5 and τ=6/5. The results μ_r=9/4, η₂=3η₀/2, and β₂=3β₁/2 are written.

For unit incident electric field amplitude, the directional power densities are: incident 1/(2η₀), reflected −1/(50η₀), transmitted 24/(50η₀) W/m². The total power is conserved.
