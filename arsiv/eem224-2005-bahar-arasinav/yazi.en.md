## Semicircular line charge
On the right semicircle with b=2 m, ρl=ρ₀cosφ, ρ₀=−16πε₀; −π/2≤φ≤π/2. The source is r′=bcosφ âx+bsinφ ây, and the observation point is the origin. In the Coulomb integral the y component vanishes by symmetry; ∫cos²φ dφ=π/2. E=πâx V/m; V=ρ₀/(2πε₀)=−8 V. The work done by an external force to bring a 10 C charge from infinity to the center at constant speed is W=qV=−80 J.

## Dielectric boundary
x<0: ε₁=ε₀; x≥0: ε₂=2.4ε₀. D₁=ε₀(3âx−4ây). E₁=3âx−4ây, P₁=0. From continuity of normal D and tangential E, D₂=ε₀(3âx−9.6ây); E₂=1.25âx−4ây; P₂=ε₀(1.75âx−5.6ây). The small angles measured from the y axis at the interface are θ₁≈37°, θ₂≈17.4°. The polarization surface charge is −1.75ε₀.

## Linearly varying dielectric
The plates are at y=1 and y=e; ε varies linearly from ε₀ at the first plate to eε₀ at the second plate, so ε(y)=ε₀y. V₀=1 V, S=1 m². D=Kây, and V₀=∫K/(ε₀y)dy gives K=ε₀.

- D=ε₀ây, E=ây/y, P=ε₀(y−1)ây/y.
- ρpv=−ε₀/y²; the polarization surface charge at the bottom is zero, and at the top it is ε₀(e−1)/e.
- The volume charge is ε₀(1/e−1), and the surface charge has the opposite sign; the total is zero.
- The free surface charge on the bottom plate is ε₀; C=ε₀ F.
