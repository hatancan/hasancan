## Coaxial Structure and Magnetization
Using Ampère's law, the solution finds Hφ = Ir/(2π) in the region r<1, Hφ = I/(2πr) in the region 1<r<2, and H = 0 outside. From the magnetization in the magnetic material, Jm = ∇×M and Jms = M×ân are computed. The equivalent volume current is 2I, the inner surface current is I, and the outer surface current is −3I; the total is zero.

The magnetic pressure at the r=1 boundary is μ₀I²/(8π²), directed along −r. The inductance per unit length is given in the source as L = 9μ₀/(8π) H/m.

## Magnetic Boundary Conditions
On the z=0 surface separating the media μr₁ = 4 and μr₂ = 3, Js = 9ây A/m; in the second medium H₂ = 14âx+8âz. Using the jump in tangential H and the continuity of normal B, H₁ = 5âx+6âz and B₁ = μ₀(20âx+24âz) are found.

## Circular Current and Charge
The field of a circular current on its axis is obtained by the Biot–Savart integral as B = [μ₀Ic²/(2(c²+h²)³ᐟ²)] ây. With c=1, h=√3, I=16/μ₀, B=ây. The field of a circular charge is computed along the axis direction; in the solution, E=âz. For a moving charge, in the expression F=q(v×B+E) the electric and magnetic terms cancel each other, and the result is zero.

## Capacitor with Position-Dependent Dielectric
For 0<y<1 there is vacuum, and in the region 1<y<2, ε(y)=4ε₀/y²; D = Kây. From the voltage integral and the given conditions, K=4ε₀. E₁=4ây, E₂=y²ây; P₂=ε₀(4−y²)ây.

- Volume polarization charge ρpv=2yε₀.
- Surface polarization charge at y=1 is −3ε₀; at y=2 it is zero.
- When the field is 19, the volume charge is 57ε₀ and the surface charge is −57ε₀; the total is zero.
- C=12ε₀, energy 722ε₀/3 J.
- The pressure at the y=1 interface is 6ε₀ N/m², in the direction of the smaller permittivity, −y.

## Conceptual Questions
Divergence is associated with the scalar source density, and curl with the vector source density. The gradient gives the direction and rate of fastest increase. In a static perfect conductor, E=0 and the potential is constant. ∇·B=0; for a steady current, the normal component of J is continuous across a boundary. The jump in the tangential component of H is related to the surface current. The curl of a static electric field is zero.
