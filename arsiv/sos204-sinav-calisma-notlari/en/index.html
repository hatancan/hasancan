SOS204 Business Administration: Midterm and Final Exam Study Questions (Topic Lists and Answer Notes)

SOS204 – İşletme · 27.04.2015 · 14.11.2026

Exam study lists shared in class for the SOS204 Business Administration course, together with a classmate's answer notes written on those lists (14 pages for the midterm, 20 pages for the final). Four documents have been combined: the midterm topic list, the final question list, and the answered versions of both. Personal information has been removed. The original PDFs can be downloaded as a ZIP at the end of the post; this post contains the question lists and excerpts from the answer notes.

Topics to study for the midterm (40 questions)

The first list was shared under the heading "Topics that will 100% come up in Business Administration"; in weekly order:

  1. Factors of production; differences between an entrepreneur and a manager; general objectives of businesses; profit.
  2. Cost concepts related to expenses: fixed, variable, total, average, marginal, alternative (opportunity), direct and indirect costs.
  3. Types of businesses (micro, small businesses, etc.), benefits of technology, macro environment and the difference between the macro and micro environment, the difference between SOEs (state economic enterprises) and PEEs (public economic establishments), types of external growth.
  4. Strategic cooperation between businesses: gentlemen's agreement, cartel, consortium, license agreement, trust.
  5. Management: duties of lower-, middle-, and upper-level employees; managerial skills; management functions; mission and vision; homeostasis; types of decisions.
  6. Development of management thought: the Hawthorne studies (the illumination, relay assembly test room, and bank wiring observation room experiments); the contemporary management era: systems approach, total quality, reengineering, contingency; cross-cultural differences (Hofstede).
  7. Planning and organizing: the difference between aim and objective, single-use plans, organizational design, span of control, the difference between leadership and management.
  8. Capital: the difference between fixed capital and working capital; paid-up and running royalty; factors affecting working capital.
  9. Methods of evaluating investment decisions: payback period, internal rate of return; depreciation and depreciation calculation methods.

Study questions for the final (65 questions, weeks 5, 9, 10, 11, 12, and 13)

  • Week 5 (establishment and investment): types of capacity (theoretical, practical, actual); the difference between technical and financial studies; sources that meet financing needs; criteria for evaluating investments (methods that take the time value of money into account and those that do not); net present value; internal rate of return; depreciation and depreciable assets; reasons that drive people to start a business; stages of an investment project; criteria for evaluating establishment work; factors in site selection; preliminary project work (economic, financial, technical, and legal studies).
  • Week 10 (marketing): the concepts of profit, cost, and competitor; distribution channels and their types (by type of relationship, by form of integration, intensive, selective, and exclusive distribution); factors affecting distribution structure; direct marketing (e.g., home shopping TV, over-the-counter sales); pricing methods (differentiation by customer, place, time, product, target price, and cost); product differentiation; differences between classical, contemporary, and social marketing; production, product, and sales concepts; the marketing mix; the difference between a marketing information system and marketing research; types of unsought goods; shopping goods and specialty goods.
  • Week 13 (finance): types of capital and financing by source (equity, external sources, self-financing); traditional and contemporary sources of financing; factoring and forfaiting; the balance sheet; the funds flow statement; current ratio; break-even analysis; speculation and manipulation.
  • Week 9 (production): types of production (handicraft, workshop, factory); production systems (flexible, computer-aided design and manufacturing); inventory control (periodic and continuous), economic order quantity; quality methods (acceptance sampling, statistical process control).
  • Week 11 (human resources): job description and job analysis; important factors in employee selection (objectivity, validity, reliability, impartiality); human resources/personnel management.
  • Week 12 (public relations): megamarketing, lobbying, sponsorship; productivity, economy, profitability, effectiveness, and efficiency; market research (desk research, surveys).

Excerpts from the answer notes

Factors of production. Traditional: natural resources (all materials obtained from nature that are transformed into products), labor (everyone who contributes physically and mentally to the production of goods and services), capital (all resources that assist production). Contemporary: the entrepreneur (the person who founds the business, bears risk, and is creative) and knowledge/knowledge resources.

Entrepreneur and manager. The entrepreneur is the person who acquires the factors of production, brings them together in an orderly manner, pursues profit, and bears all the risks of the venture. The manager, on the other hand, brings the factors of production together and takes on the responsibility of running the established business in line with its purpose, with the profit and the risk belonging to others.

Types of capacity. Theoretical (maximum) capacity: the highest output the existing system can deliver without any interruption; practical (planned) capacity: the output that can be delivered with normal, predetermined interruptions; actual (realized) capacity: the output realized at the end of a given period as a result of internal and external conditions (market demand, strikes).

Technical and financial studies. Technical: site selection, suitability for construction, resistance to earthquakes and natural disasters, product characteristics, machinery-equipment and layout plan, number and qualifications of employees, occupational safety, prevention of environmental pollution, need for licenses and patents. Financial: the need for establishment and fixed capital, the need for working capital, determining investment expenditures and financing needs.

Sources of financing. Equity (the entrepreneur's or partners' own assets), external sources (borrowing), and self-financing (profits retained rather than distributed in an operating business).

Investment appraisal. Methods that take the time value of money into account assume that the value of net cash inflows is not equal across years (net present value, internal rate of return); those that do not take it into account make no distinction between the first and the last year (payback period, profit/investment ratio). Net present value is the difference between the sum of the net cash inflows the investment will generate over its economic life, discounted to the present at a predetermined discount rate, and the cash outflows the investment requires.

The rest of the notes (answers to questions on distribution channels, pricing methods, the balance sheet, the funds flow statement, break-even analysis, inventory control, economic order quantity, etc.) are in the documents in the ZIP.
