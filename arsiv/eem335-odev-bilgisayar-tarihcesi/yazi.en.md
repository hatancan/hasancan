## Assignment 1: History of Computers

The history of computers is divided into two periods, the **Mechanical Era** and the **Electronic Era**; the electronic era consists of 5 generations.

1. **Mechanical era:** People first counted with fingers and pebbles, and then invented the **abacus** (by the Chinese around 1000 BC).
2. **1st generation (1946–1959):** Vacuum tubes and the idea of storing the program in memory formed the basis of modern computer architecture. The era begins with **ENIAC**, developed by Eckert and Mauchly; it consumed a lot of power, got very hot, and had a speed of 10 KIPS; it could calculate in 20 seconds the trajectory of a mortar shell that would reach its target in 30 seconds. Software required detailed machine language.
3. **2nd generation (1959–1964):** **Transistors** instead of tubes; computers became smaller and faster (about 10,000 transistors, 32 KB of memory, 200 KIPS); high-level languages (Fortran, Basic); an example is the IBM 7094. The first transistor dates from 1948.
4. **3rd generation (1964–1970):** **Integrated circuits**; 2 MB of memory and 5 MIPS; examples are the IBM 360 and 370.
5. **4th generation (1970–):** **Microprocessors**; home computers; 30 MIPS and 8 MB of memory; disks and CDs became widespread; an example is the IBM PC.
6. **5th generation:** research on artificial intelligence and computers capable of controlling themselves.

## Assignment: History of Computers – From the Abacus to ENIAC

The second work grounds the history in the need to count numbers, noting that the word "computer" derives from the root "compute": the abacus, used in Eastern civilizations around 1000 BC, is regarded as the ancestor of the computer. It then describes Pascal's adding machine, Colmar's "Arithometer", Charles Babbage's **Difference Engine**, designed for differential equations, and his general-purpose **Analytical Engine** (using punched cards, inspired by Jacquard's 1801 punched wooden board/card design), and arrives at ENIAC.
