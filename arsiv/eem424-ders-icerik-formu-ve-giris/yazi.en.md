Gökhun Tanyer** in the Department of Electrical and Electronics Engineering at Başkent University: the course content form (2013–2014 spring), the weekly planning presentation, and the introduction to microwaves presentation (2014–2015 spring). ## Course content form (2013–2014 spring)

- **Course:** EEM 424 Microwave Theory; **instructor:** Prof. Dr. S. Gökhun Tanyer; target success rate 95%.
- **Textbook:** D. M. Pozar (2012), *Microwave Engineering*, Wiley; R. E. Collin, *Foundations for Microwave Engineering*; D. K. Cheng, *Field and Wave Electromagnetics* and *Fundamentals of Engineering Electromagnetics*.
- **References/supplementary:** N. Kampfer and A. Murk (Microwave Physics and Quasioptics: Introduction); R. M. O'Donnell (Radar Systems Engineering, IEEE, 2009); L. Schachter (Microwaves); F. K. W. Lee (Microwave Filters); S.-O. Park (Microwave Engineering).
- **Grading:** quizzes 25%, homework 10%, midterm exam 35%, final exam 30% (total 100%).

**Weekly topics:**

1. EM I, II; vector algebra, operators, coordinate systems; differential equations for wave analysis; normal and oblique incidence of plane EM waves on planar surfaces; total reflection, surface waves.
2. Transmission lines and examples; basic transmission line example: parallel plate; wave properties of the infinite transmission line; attenuation constant, power.
3. Coaxial line parameters (Pozar); transmission lines as circuit elements; transients on transmission lines, reflection diagrams, pulse excitation.
4. Smith chart; basic Smith chart operations; ZY Smith chart; lossy lines; impedance measurement with a slotted line.
5. Smith chart, impedance matching; quarter-wave transformer; generator and load mismatch; conjugate matching.
6. Smith chart, double-stub matching; waveguides; general solutions.
7. TEM, TE and TM waves.
8. **Midterm exam**; wave impedances.
9. Parallel plate waveguide (TE, TM); energy, power, attenuation; rectangular waveguides (TM and TE); attenuation.
10. Partially loaded waveguide, flanges; circular waveguides; dielectric waveguides, dielectric slab; coaxial lines; surface waves on a grounded dielectric slab; stripline.
11. Microstrip transmission line; wave velocities and dispersion; group velocity; power capacity of transmission lines; microwave network analysis (impedance and equivalent V and I, impedance and admittance matrices, scattering matrix).
12. Transmission (ABCD) matrix; signal flow graphs; modal analysis; excitation of waveguides; rectangular cavity resonators; TE_mnp modes.
13. Quality factor of a resonator; circular cavity resonator.
14. **Final exam** (3 February 2014).

## Weekly planning presentation (HDPD)

The presentation first explains the motivation for the course: in EM I courses, electric and magnetic fields were treated separately, and in EM II they were seen unified through Maxwell's equations, as an EM wave over the frequency range [0, ∞) (including light). The reflection and transmission properties observed when different materials are brought together can be related to electronic circuits; if circuit elements have lengths comparable to the wavelength of the transmission line they are connected to, the equivalent impedance can be very different and simple circuit rules do not work. The microwave theory course picks up exactly at this point: transmission lines, waveguides, resonators, and microwave components. In the near future, knowledge of "RF engineering" is expected to concern all engineers.

Course schedule: 17.02.2014 – 23.05.2014 (Monday 15:00–16:50, Thursday 11:00–12:50; office hours Thursday 14:00–16:50); final exam week 24.05 – 04.06.2014. Weekly homework is assigned and is due one week later (the following Monday); late submissions lose 20% of the points for every 24 hours. There is one midterm exam, a project in place of the final exam, and possible short quizzes; there is no make-up exam. Prerequisites: MAT 151–152, EEM 224 Electromagnetics I, EEM 323 Electromagnetics II; Euclidean geometry, complex analysis, linear systems, MATLAB, basic electrodynamics, wave propagation, Fourier analysis. Additional textbooks: P. Russer, A. A. Smith, Y. Shmaliy, K. Kurokawa; MIT OpenCourseWare courses.

## Introduction to microwaves presentation (2014–2015 spring)

The 39-slide introduction presentation consists mostly of figures (the slides cite S.-O. Park, R. M. O'Donnell, K. Chen, Kampfer and Murk, and Nikolova as sources). Topics: review of EM I and II; the **electromagnetic spectrum**; **why microwaves?**; frequency band allocations (Homework 1: find similar allocations for Turkey); the analogy between RF and optics; the analogy between biological and RF solutions; other applications of microwaves (Kampfer, Murk): measurement and sensing applications, radiometers (ionospheric sounder) and imaging radar. SAR (noted as studied in EEM 538); Homework: find a document about ASAR and explain what you understood.
