## Course objectives

- To understand the principles and abstractions of digital electronics that form the basis of digital system design, including TTL and CMOS digital systems.
- To use these engineering abstractions to analyze and design simple digital circuits.
- To build digital logic gates and measure parameters such as propagation delay, noise margins, and fan-out using equipment such as the CADET training board, FLUKE 179 multimeter, Goodwill GPC-3030 variable DC power supply, function generator, oscilloscopes, and digital integrated circuits. To compare the measurements with the predicted mathematical behavior models and explain the differences.
- To understand the relationship between the mathematical representation of circuit behavior and the corresponding real-world effects.
- To obtain the V-I characteristics of TTL and CMOS inverters and determine their noise margins.
- To understand the operation of various memory units.
- To implement multivibrator/timing circuits with specified duty cycles.
- To understand the operation and principles of BiCMOS and GaAs circuits.
- To learn VLSI fabrication techniques.
- To grasp the practical importance of the systems developed in the course.

## Course learning outcomes

Students who successfully complete this course will be able to:

- Use Boolean algebra to describe the function of logic circuits.
- Design circuits that represent digital logic expressions. In particular, design a gate-level digital circuit to implement a given Boolean function.
- Build and analyze TTL/CMOS digital circuits, and evaluate their performance by measuring propagation delay, noise margins, and fan-out.
- Understand the operation and principles of BiCMOS and GaAs circuits.
- Design combinational and sequential circuits using TTL and CMOS integrated circuits.
- Recognize the importance and advantages of CMOS technology.

## Course content

Semiconductors, operation of the p-n junction. Operation of the diode and BJT. BJT switching characteristics. Introduction to digital integrated circuits. Logic components with DTL, TTL, ECL, and fan-in/fan-out. CMOS operation, logic components with CMOS. BiCMOS and GaAs circuits. Multivibrators and pulse generators. Static and dynamic memory cells. Thermal effects and power dissipation in integrated circuits. VLSI fabrication techniques.
